
INDUSTRIAL MAINTENANCE GUIDE

1. MOTOR MAINTENANCE

Common motor problems include overheating, unusual vibration, excessive noise, and failure to start.

Possible causes of overheating include blocked ventilation, a damaged cooling fan, excessive load, and poor maintenance.

Maintenance guidance:
- Stop the equipment if overheating is suspected.
- Notify the responsible maintenance personnel.
- Only trained and authorized personnel should inspect the motor.
- Isolate the energy source and follow the site's lockout/tagout procedure before inspection or maintenance.
- Never touch exposed electrical parts.

2. CONVEYOR BELT MAINTENANCE

Common conveyor problems include belt misalignment, unusual noise, slipping, and visible wear.

Maintenance guidance:
- Stop the conveyor if an unsafe condition is observed.
- Report belt damage or misalignment to maintenance personnel.
- Keep people away from moving belts and rollers.
- Isolate energy and follow lockout/tagout procedures before inspection or maintenance.
- Do not attempt repairs while the conveyor is running.

3. PUMP MAINTENANCE

Common pump problems include leakage, unusual vibration, overheating, and reduced flow.

Maintenance guidance:
- Stop and report any serious or unusual leakage.
- Check for visible leaks only from a safe distance.
- Inspection and repairs must be carried out by trained personnel.
- Isolate electrical and other hazardous energy sources before maintenance.
- Follow the manufacturer's instructions and site procedures.

4. MACHINE NOISE AND VIBRATION

Unusual noise or vibration can indicate loose components, worn parts, misalignment, or another mechanical problem.

Maintenance guidance:
- Stop the machine if it appears unsafe.
- Record the symptoms and report them to the maintenance team.
- Do not remove guards or touch moving components.
- Inspection must follow site safety procedures.

5. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE

Preventive maintenance helps reduce unexpected equipment failures.

Recommended practices:
- Follow the manufacturer's maintenance schedule.
- Check equipment for visible damage from a safe position.
- Keep maintenance records.
- Report unusual sounds, vibration, overheating, and leakage.
- Allow only trained and authorized personnel to perform maintenance.

IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTICE

This guide provides general educational information. It does not replace manufacturer instructions, approved site procedures, or professional maintenance advice. Never perform maintenance on energized or moving machinery.
